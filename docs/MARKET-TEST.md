# 90-Day Downstate Market Test

## Thesis

Riverdale and Lower Westchester are a better first Downstate test than Manhattan proper because the territory includes more residential properties, lawns, schools, driveways, and event spaces that can use Friendly Party Rental's existing inventory mix.

## What we are testing

We are not testing whether people in New York have parties. We are testing whether Friendly Party Rental can acquire **profitable, serviceable Downstate orders** at a cost and average order value that justify a permanent local operation.

## Phase 1 — Days 1–30: Demand

- Launch Riverdale-first site.
- Run tightly geofenced search ads.
- Separate campaigns by Riverdale/Bronx and Lower Westchester.
- Track every quote request by source, ZIP/neighborhood, date, requested equipment, and estimated order value.
- Do not broaden to all NYC just to create volume.

## Phase 2 — Days 31–60: Conversion + route economics

Measure:
- qualified leads
- quotes sent
- booked events
- average booked order
- delivery/setup labor hours
- tolls/fuel/parking
- equipment requested but unavailable
- cancellation rate
- gross contribution after direct job costs

## Phase 3 — Days 61–90: Expansion decision

Consider local storage/warehouse only if the constraint becomes **travel from Syracuse**, rather than lack of profitable Downstate demand.

## Internal starting hypotheses — not promises

- Delivered-order starting target: about $750+ depending on zone and setup.
- Prefer fewer higher-value jobs over small orders that consume a full truck route.
- Premium wedding/event packages should be tested alongside backyard/family rentals.
- Pole tents should only be sold where the site and anchoring are appropriate.
- Manhattan should remain a selective premium market, not the core of this first test.

## Scorecard

At day 90 answer:

1. How many qualified Downstate leads did we receive?
2. Which three neighborhoods produced the most profitable requests?
3. What was booked revenue and direct job contribution?
4. What was average booked order value?
5. What percentage of leads needed equipment we already own?
6. How many jobs were lost because Syracuse was too far away?
7. How many were lost because the customer could not meet the delivery/minimum threshold?
8. Did ad spend produce repeatable customer acquisition?
9. Is a Mount Vernon/Yonkers-area base operationally justified?
10. Should we continue, narrow, expand, or stop the test?
